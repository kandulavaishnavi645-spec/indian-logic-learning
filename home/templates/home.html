Indian Logic and
Philosophy

Based on M. Hiriyanna’s Outlines of Indian Philosophy

Introduction

Indian philosophy is a rich tradition of systematic thought that explores reality, knowledge and the means to attain valid knowledge. Among its six classical schools (darśanas), Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika are especially important to the study of logic and reasoning.

The Six Systems of Indian Philosophy

Nyāya

Logic and valid knowledge

Vaiśeṣika

Categories of reality

Sāṃkhya

Nature and consciousness

Yoga

Discipline and liberation

Mīmāṃsā

Ritual and interpretation

Vedānta

Self and ultimate reality

Nyāya Philosophy

  • Founded by Gautama (Akṣapāda).
  • Focuses on logic, reasoning and the means of valid knowledge (pramāṇas).
  • Develops methods to analyse arguments and identify fallacies.

The Four Pramāṇas (Means of Valid Knowledge)

Pratyakṣa

Perception through the senses.

◉

Anumāna

Inference from evidence and reasoning.

Upamāna

Knowing through comparison and similarity.

Śabda

The Five-Part Nyāya Argument (Syllogism)

  1. Pratijñā — The proposition (e.g., The hill has fire).
  2. Hetu — The reason (e.g., Because it has smoke).
  3. Udāharaṇa — A general example (e.g., Wherever there is smoke, there is fire).
  4. Upanaya — Application to the case (e.g., The hill has smoke).
  5. Nigamana — The conclusion (e.g., Therefore, the hill has fire).

Vaiśeṣika Philosophy

  • Founded by Kaṇāda.
  • Focuses on the classification of reality into categories (padārthas).
  • Investigates the nature of substances such as substance, quality, action, generality, particularity and inherence.

Relation Between
Indian Logic and
Modern Computing

Based on Dr. V. S. Sarma’s research paper, “A Survey of Indian Logic from the View Point of Computer Science”.

Overview

V. S. Sarma (1994) explores how the principles of Indian logic, especially Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika, can be compared in relation to modern computer science and Artificial Intelligence (AI). The paper shows that ideas about knowledge, reasoning and classification in Indian logic can be compared with knowledge-based intelligent systems.

Example: Medical Expert System (MYCIN)

The paper discusses MYCIN as an early medical expert system. It can compare symptoms entered by a clinician with knowledge and rules, then provide a possible recommendation for professional review.

This is an illustrative comparison between rule-based computing and structured reasoning—not a claim that the two traditions are identical.

Nyāya Syllogism and Computer Reasoning

The Nyāya syllogism explains how a conclusion follows from a reason and an example.

  1. Pratijñā — The hill has fire.
  2. Hetu — Because it has smoke.
  3. Udāharaṇa — Where there is smoke, there is fire.
  4. Upanaya — The hill has smoke.
  5. Nigamana — Therefore, the hill has fire.
if smoke(hill)
and smoke_implies_fire:
    conclude(fire(hill))

Key Takeaways from the Research Paper

  • Indian logic provides systematic accounts of knowledge and inference.
  • Knowledge-based AI systems use structured facts and rules to process information.
  • Comparisons illuminate similarities while respecting the differences between the traditions.
